Research Overview & Objectives

Urban road networks face compounding pressures from rising traffic density, infrastructure degradation, and emergency delays. This project provides an end-to-end data visualization and econometric framework to answer a critical municipal policy question:

"What spatial, infrastructure, and environmental factors predict accident-prone urban zones, and where should municipal infrastructure funding be targeted?"

Econometric Logistic Regression (Collision Probability)

To go beyond simple correlations, we estimate a Binary Logistic Regression evaluating the likelihood of an accident occurring ($\text{accident\_reported} = 1$) conditioned on built-environment and infrastructure attributes.

Econometric Insights:
  • Road Condition: Poor road status increases collision odds by more than 2.1 times holding traffic volume constant.
  • Active Workzones: Road construction presence shows a statistically significant positive effect (p < 0.01) on accident exposure.

Non-Linear Environmental Impact & Delay Dynamics

Evaluating non-linear operational friction by plotting Rainfall Intensity (mm) against Average Wait Time, segmented by collision occurrence (accident_reported = 1) and overlaid with Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) trendlines.

Key Econometric Insights:
  • Severe Wait Time Outliers (Accident Clustering): Delays above 300 seconds (peaking near 1,200s) consist almost exclusively of confirmed accident events (bright yellow markers), proving that collisions cause extreme tail-risk operational delays rather than routine weather slowdowns.
  • Dry-Weather Sensitivity ($0\text{ mm}$ Baseline): A massive vertical concentration of delays occurs at 0 mm rainfall. This indicates that baseline urban traffic volume and corridor structural capacity—not precipitation alone—drive the bulk of everyday congestion[cite: 1].
  • Near-Zero Baseline OLS Slope: The fitted red OLS regression line remains nearly flat across rainfall levels[cite: 1]. Precipitation operates as a non-linear volatility catalyst during active collisions rather than a steady linear multiplier on standard wait times[cite: 1].
  • Policy Action: Incident management units should prioritize rapid clearance during minor precipitation (0--10 mm), where collision-induced delays spike exponentially above network norms[cite: 1].
